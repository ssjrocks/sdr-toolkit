**Decode points follow their signal (AFC)**

- DMR / P25 decode points now tune themselves onto their signal, as a radio's automatic frequency control (AFC) does. This covers points you add and the ones the explorer finds. Each point measures how far its signal sits from its centre and moves part of the way every half second, up to 4 kHz either way. It keeps following a transmitter or dongle that drifts as it warms up.
- It only adjusts while the spectrum shows a signal there (10 dB over the noise), so noise doesn't push a point around. It works even when the signal is too far off to decode yet.
- On the spectrum, a point's line moves with it, and its label shows how far it has moved once that's 0.3 kHz or more ("DMR +2.5 kHz").
- The bandwidth isn't adjusted: DMR and P25 are about 9 kHz wide and fit the 12.5 kHz channel.

Tested on generated DMR in a recording. A point placed 2.5 kHz below its signal moved onto it (within 400 Hz) and then decoded frames. A point on a signal drifting 1.5 kHz followed it. A point on noise stayed put. Not yet tried live.
