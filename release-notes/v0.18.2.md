**HackRF: on this PC, a bar with automatic gain, sweeps, and the problems found on a real one**

**A HackRF plugged into this PC now works.** Until now the toolkit could use a HackRF only through SDR Tools Relay on another PC. Plug one in and every SDR mode uses it: the relay button says **HackRF on this PC**. It runs through a built-in copy of the relay's own server, which listens on this PC only. Its menu sets the HackRF aside for this PC's RTL-SDR until you replug it; unplug it and the modes go back to the RTL-SDR by themselves. On Linux it needs libhackrf; the toolkit offers to install it.

**The HackRF bar** replaces the HackRF… dialog. It sits on the SDR pages, and every change reaches the HackRF at once, on whatever is streaming from it. (In 0.17 a change sometimes needed a toggle before it applied: each stream had copied the settings when it opened.)
- **Auto gain or Manual gain.** A HackRF has no gain control of its own, so the toolkit makes one. Once a second it reads the input level over the whole band and steps the LNA and VGA to keep it in the green band of the **level meter**. It backs off at once if samples clip. On a real HackRF it brought a quiet band from -39 to -30 dBFS within about 10 seconds.
- **Amp** and **Antenna power**.
- **Filter**, for each page separately. A narrow filter set for DMR no longer blacks out the receiver's wide view at 19.2 MS/s. That was the "noise in the centre" at high rates.

**The centre spike is gone.** A HackRF shows a tall spike at the centre of every view (about 45 dB). It's now removed before the samples reach the decoders: by the relay from SDR Tools Relay 0.3.2, by the built-in one here, and by the toolkit itself for older relays. On the real HackRF, all that's left at the centre is its own broad 4 dB rise.

**Survey uses the HackRF's own sweep:** 1 MHz to 6 GHz in 0.75 s, in 19.5 kHz bins.

**More rates and honest meters:**
- Spectrum & receiver offers 4.8 to 19.2 MS/s on a HackRF.
- Modes that want less than its 2 MS/s are narrowed at the relay, so less crosses the network.
- The network meter says when samples are being lost, and how much the link carries. Each MS/s is 2 MB/s, so 19.2 MS/s needs gigabit Ethernet; a 100 Mbit link carries about 5.5.
- The USB meter shows the HackRF's own overrun count.

**Fixes:**
- Survey on a HackRF crashed the receiver; found in the live test.
- Status lines said "the RTL-SDR" for a HackRF, or showed an apostrophe as "&#x27;".
- Narrowed streams (from older relays, and the built-in relay's copy from SDR Tools Relay 0.3.3) could keep half a step of DC on a quiet input; the rounding is now dithered. Found by the Windows build's tests, which kept 0.18.0 from being released.
- With a relay older than 0.3.0, the toolkit's own DC spike removal could swap I and Q after a network chunk of odd length (common on Windows), leaving the spike in. Found by the same tests, which kept 0.18.1 back.

Tested live on a HackRF plugged into this PC, with Spectrum & receiver, auto gain and Survey. Not tested live: transmit (nothing in the toolkit sends yet), and the full set of decoding modes on the HackRF.
