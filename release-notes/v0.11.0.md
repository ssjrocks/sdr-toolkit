**Radiosondes: weather balloons**

A new **Radiosondes** mode under RTL-SDR follows weather balloons' radiosondes on 400-406 MHz.

- **Finds the sondes:** the dongle watches 2.2 MHz of the band, and any narrow signal well above the noise is tried. rs1729's `dft_detect` identifies the sonde type and its polarity, and that type's decoder from [rs1729/RS](https://github.com/rs1729/RS) decodes it: RS41, DFM, M10/M20, RS92, iMet-54, Meisei and others. Several sondes are followed at once, each in its own channel. You can also give the frequencies yourself.
- **The table:** serial, type, frequency, altitude, climb, speed, heading, temperature, humidity, pressure, battery, GPS satellites and distance from you.
- **The map:** each balloon with its track. Once it's coming down, a cross marks a rough landing estimate (a straight line at its descent rate on its current wind), with when and how far from you it should land. Details links to the sonde on SondeHub, which predicts landings from wind forecasts.
- Sondes go into the logbook like aircraft and ships. It works with this PC's dongle, a relay or a recording.
- **Install:** the decoders are built from source by the installer (Install on the mode's page). On Windows they have no release yet.

In Australia the Bureau of Meteorology launches Vaisala RS41s around 9 am and 9 pm local time, on about 400.5-403.5 MHz.

**Tested:** a real RS41 recording from the decoders' repository (sonde K1930308, 2014), turned into the dongle's IQ: two copies at once were found, identified as RS41 and decoded, and the mode's table, map, log and details were checked, in the window too. **Not yet tried:** a live sonde with a dongle, and the other sonde types (no recordings of them decode with these decoders).
